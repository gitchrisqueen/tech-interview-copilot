# Behavioral answers: STAR that lands

## The shape (60-90 seconds)

- **Situation** (1-2 sentences): context, team size, stakes. Name the constraint.
- **Task** (1 sentence): what YOU owned. "I was responsible for..."
- **Action** (the meat, 3-5 sentences): the decisions you made and why. First person singular -
  "I profiled, I proposed, I convinced" - not "we".
- **Result** (1-2 sentences): a number if at all possible (latency, revenue, adoption, time
  saved), plus what you learned or would do differently.

## Prepare 6 stories, tag each with the questions it answers

| Story slot | Covers |
|---|---|
| A hard technical problem you solved | "most challenging project", "debugging war story" |
| A conflict or disagreement | "disagreed with a teammate/manager", "pushback" |
| A failure or mistake | "tell me about a failure", "what would you do differently" |
| Leading or influencing without authority | "leadership", "mentoring", "driving alignment" |
| Delivering under pressure / scope cut | "tight deadline", "prioritization" |
| Initiative beyond the job | "went above and beyond", "improved a process" |

## Do / avoid

- Do answer the question asked, then stop. Silence is the interviewer's job to fill.
- Do own failures cleanly: what you missed, what changed afterward. No blame-shifting.
- Avoid "we" for your contribution, avoid trashing former employers, avoid stories with no
  outcome, avoid rambling past 2 minutes.

## Questions to ask them (pick 2-3)

- What does the first 90 days look like for this role?
- What separates a good hire from a great one on this team a year in?
- What is the team's biggest technical bet right now, and what worries you about it?
- How does the team handle incidents / tech debt / design review?
